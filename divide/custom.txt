-------------
CUSTOMIZATION
-------------
This is the DIVISION game, served at https://multiply.by/divide/. It is the same game as the rest
of this file (the same modes, score, streak, timer, menu, rules, animations, sounds, "You Won!"
screen and saved progress), except that every question is a division: "56 ÷ 8", answer 7. Each
multiplication fact of the dataset is one FACT FAMILY: 7 x 8 = 56 gives 56 ÷ 7 = 8 and
56 ÷ 8 = 7.

DATASET
- Keep exactly the same 120 facts in exactly the same order and with the same ids 1 to 120 (saved
  progress refers to the ids), but give each fact these fields:
  id | text (the multiplication, e.g. "7 x 8": the fact's name) | a (the smaller factor, 7) |
  b (the larger factor, 8) | product (56)
  a, b and product are numbers. The dataset has no "answer" field.
- `function factors(f) { return [f.a, f.b]; }` (replaces the version that splits the text), so
  applyMode chooses the same facts as before: 36 for 2 to 9, 48 for up to 12, 116 for up to 20.
- The deck, learnedIds, answerLog, weakFacts and everything else that stores or finds a fact keep
  using the fact objects of FACTS and their ids, as before.

DEALING A QUESTION (CRITICAL)
- Each time `nextQuestion()` deals a fact, it picks the divisor at random, a or b (each with
  probability 1/2; when a equals b, as in 6 x 6, there is only one), and puts on screen a COPY of
  the fact with the division as its text and the quotient as its answer:
  `const d = Math.random() < 0.5 ? fact.a : fact.b;`
  `state.question = { ...fact, text: fact.product + ' ÷ ' + d, answer: String(fact.product / d), divisor: d };`
  The ÷ is the division sign (U+00F7), with one space on each side. The copy keeps the fact's id,
  so learning, missed questions, ANSWER TIMES and PRACTICE HARD ONES all work on the fact family:
  a fact is learned when either of its divisions is answered right. A missed fact comes back at
  the end of the deck and, when it is dealt again, picks its divisor again at random.
- Always build the text and the answer from a, b and product, never by reading another question's
  text (a fact moved back into the deck may be the on-screen copy).
- The question card shows `state.question.text` (e.g. "56 ÷ 8"); the right answer is
  `state.question.answer` (e.g. "7"). Everything else that the rest of this file says about
  `fact.text` and `fact.answer` of the question on screen means these two copy fields.

WRONG ANSWERS (CRITICAL: replaces the whole WRONG ANSWERS rule of the multiplication game)
- `wrongAnswers(q)` (q is the question on screen) returns exactly three answer strings, all
  different from each other and from `q.answer`. ans = Number(q.answer), p = q.product,
  d = q.divisor.
- RANGE: every wrong answer is between 2 and the mode's largest factor (9 for 2 to 9, 12 for up to
  12, 20 for up to 20), so no choice can be ruled out because no fact of the set has that answer
  (no 1, and no 10 or more in 2 to 9).
- SAME ODD/EVEN: when p is odd, the answer is odd, so every wrong answer MUST be odd; when p is even
  and d is odd, the answer is even, so every wrong answer MUST be even; when p and d are both even,
  wrong answers may be odd or even. Otherwise the player could find the answer by its parity.
- Candidates: BELOW = the allowed numbers smaller than ans, nearest first (ans - 1, ans - 2, ...,
  keeping only those that pass the two rules above); ABOVE = the allowed numbers bigger than ans,
  nearest first.
- Pick k, the number of wrong answers below the right one, at random from 0, 1, 2 and 3, keeping
  only values where BELOW has at least k entries and ABOVE has at least 3 - k. Return the first k
  of BELOW and the first 3 - k of ABOVE, as strings. (So the right answer is the smallest,
  second, third or largest of the four numbers, at random, as far as the range allows.)
- Examples: 56 ÷ 8 (ans 7, p even, d even): BELOW 6, 5, 4, ... ABOVE 8, 9; k = 1 gives 6, 8, 9.
  63 ÷ 7 (ans 9, p odd) in 2 to 9: BELOW 7, 5, 3, ABOVE none: only k = 3, giving 7, 5, 3.
  48 ÷ 3 (ans 16, p even, d odd) in up to 20: BELOW 14, 12, 10, ABOVE 18, 20; k = 2 gives 14, 12,
  18. The range always leaves enough candidates: in 2 to 9 there are four odd and four even
  numbers.

SPEECH
- `speakCard` (never called, kept defined): `const text = fact.product + ' divided by ' +
  fact.divisor + ' is ' + fact.answer;` ("56 divided by 8 is 7"). Never pass the raw "÷" to the
  voice.

CHECKS for this game (in addition to the checks of the rest of this file)
- Every question reads "P ÷ D" with the division sign, and both divisions of a fact turn up over
  several deals (for example 56 ÷ 7 and 56 ÷ 8); 6 x 6 always shows 36 ÷ 6.
- Each set has 36, 48 or 116 facts; Learned reaches 100% and "You Won!" appears after every fact
  family is answered right once (36 right answers in 2 to 9, final score 990 with no mistakes).
- Wrong answers: never 1, never above the mode's largest factor, all odd when the dividend is odd,
  all even when the dividend is even and the divisor odd, and the right answer is in every
  position (smallest to largest) over many questions where the range allows.
- Saved progress is separate from the multiplication game's (GAME is "divide"); the sound setting
  is shared.
