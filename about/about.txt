<h2>How the site was built</h2>
Every page has a SOURCE button in the upper right corner. It opens the page's
<code>build-instructions.txt</code>: the plain-text prompt given to an AI model, which
writes the single file <code>index.html</code> that your browser shows. Nobody edits that file
by hand: to change a page, change its build instructions and build it again.

<h2>Make your own version</h2>
The game's build instructions are meant to be changed. Rewrite the parts you want (the
encouragement, the labels, the look) and give the file to an AI model to build your own game.
The <a href="https://multiply.by/chinese/">Chinese version</a> was made this way, with spoken
Chinese encouragement and Chinese labels.

<h2>Portability</h2>
The build instructions are written to work with models from Google, Anthropic or OpenAI, and
with open-weights models like Kimi. On cheaper models some glitches can be expected.

<h2>Scalability</h2>
The site is static, with no accounts and no server code, so it can cheaply handle large loads.
The game is a single file of less than 60 KB: it loads quickly even on a slow connection
and costs almost nothing to serve. Once installed, it runs offline, and the number of players can
grow without slowing the site down.

<h2>Learned Everywhere</h2>
What children are expected to memorize depends mostly on where they grow up. In different 
countries, religions and cultures, a child may recite prayers, learn poems by national writers, 
say a pledge of allegiance every morning, or sing the national anthem.

But there is one memorization task that is the same everywhere: the multiplication table. 
Children learn it at least up to 9; in Britain they go <a href="https://multiply.by/?12">up to 12</a>, and in India often
<a href="https://multiply.by/?20">up to 20</a>.
